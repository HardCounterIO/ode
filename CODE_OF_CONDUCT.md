# Code of Conduct

Open Detection Engineering exists to enable practitioners to collaboratively build high-quality cybersecurity education. Participation requires professional and constructive behavior.

## Expected Behavior

Participants should:
- treat others with respect
- critique ideas rather than people
- provide useful technical feedback
- assume good faith where reasonable
- acknowledge mistakes and corrections
- disclose relevant conflicts of interest
- respect contributor attribution
- help maintain a technically rigorous environment

Technical disagreement is expected and encouraged. Personal attacks are not.

## Unacceptable Behavior

The following are not acceptable:
- harassment or discrimination
- threats or intimidation
- repeated personal attacks
- deliberate disruption of project discussions
- plagiarism
- knowingly submitting proprietary or confidential material
- undisclosed commercial promotion presented as neutral guidance
- manipulating project content primarily to benefit a vendor, employer, or commercial interest

## Vendor Participation

Employees and representatives of commercial organizations are welcome. However, relevant affiliations should be disclosed when contributing material directly related to an employer's products or services. Expertise is valuable. Advertising is not.

## Enforcement

Project maintainers may edit, reject, or remove contributions and may restrict participation when behavior materially harms the project or its community.

Report concerns privately to <tj@tallisjordan.com>. Until a formal moderation process exists, reports are handled by the Project Lead. If the report concerns the Project Lead, send it to a listed maintainer instead.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies to ODE repositories, issue discussions, pull requests, project communication channels, and project-organized activities.
